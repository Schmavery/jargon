**yak shaving** *n.* [J](http://www.catb.org/jargon/html/Y/yak-shaving.html) The phenomenon of doing seemingly unrelated preparatory work that leads further and further from the original task. You want to write a blog post, which requires updating your site, which requires a working local dev environment, which requires upgrading a dependency, which requires a newer version of Node, which requires reconfiguring your shell. Hours later you are shaving a yak. The term was coined by Carlin Vieri at MIT in the 1990s and popularized by Seth Godin and the cartoon series *Ren & Stimpy*, though the exact etymology is murky. It describes a real cognitive trap: each step feels necessary and logical, but the chain of prerequisites can grow indefinitely. Differs from bikeshedding in that yak shaving is usually solitary and well-intentioned — the work feels genuinely necessary rather than trivially distracting. The question to ask is whether the prerequisite actually needs doing, or whether there's a shorter path to the original goal.  #AI-Gen
