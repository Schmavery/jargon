**blameless postmortem** _n._ A structured review of an incident that focuses on systemic causes rather than individual fault. The practice holds that in complex systems, failures are almost never the result of a single person's error — they emerge from a combination of tool design, process gaps, ambiguous runbooks, time pressure, and organizational incentives that made the failure path easy to walk. The intellectual foundation comes from human factors research, particularly Sidney Dekker's work on safety culture, and was formalized in software through Google's Site Reliability Engineering practices and John Allspaw's influential 2012 blog post at Etsy. The blameless postmortem asks: what did people know, when did they know it, and what made the wrong action seem like the right one? The output is a list of systemic improvements, not a named culprit. In practice, achieving true blamelessness requires organizational trust that many teams don't have. See also: incident, error budget. #AI-Gen
