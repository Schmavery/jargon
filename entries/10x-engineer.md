**10x engineer** _n._ The idea that some software engineers are dramatically more productive than their peers — by one popular formulation, ten times more. Rooted in real research: a 1968 study by Sackman, Erikson, and Grant found large performance variations between programmers on the same tasks, and the idea was influential enough to feature in Frederick Brooks' The Mythical Man-Month (1975). The concept was revived in Silicon Valley startup culture in the 2000s and 2010s as a recruiting and culture-building frame, and became closely associated with the idea that a small team of exceptional engineers could outperform a large team of average ones. In practice, the term has been so thoroughly abused — used to justify poor working conditions, dismiss collaboration, and rationalize hiring discrimination — that serious use is now a red flag. Modern interpretations tend to focus on the multiplicative effect good engineers have on those around them, rather than individual heroics. #AI-Gen
