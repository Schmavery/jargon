**bikeshedding** _n._ The tendency for a group to spend disproportionate time on trivial or easy-to-understand details while neglecting more complex, important ones. Named after an example in C. Northcote Parkinson's 1957 book Parkinson's Law: a committee approving plans for a nuclear power plant spends most of its meeting debating the design of a staff bike shed, because the reactor is too complex for most people to have opinions on, while everyone can picture a bike shed. Popularized in software culture by Poul-Henning Kamp's 1999 email to the FreeBSD mailing list, which became one of the most-cited pieces of programming writing. In practice it shows up in design reviews, code reviews, and sprint planning — the variable name gets thirty comments, the architecture decision gets two. See also: yak shaving. #AI-Gen
